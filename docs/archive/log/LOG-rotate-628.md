## 2026-08-08 — A hero test that was really asking what time it is (`.613`)

**`workout complete updates Mission Score on Today` had no clock, so it asserted a layout that Today only produces before 18:00 — and `.612` blamed three innocent commits for it.**

The mechanism, reproduced on demand rather than argued: Today's shell spends a budget of `TODAY_MAX_TOP_LEVEL_BLOCKS = 6`, and `dashboard` is priority **32**. `day-review` is **15** and mounts from `DAY_REVIEW_START_HOUR = 18`; `week-recap` is **30** and mounts when the week has activity *or it is the week-end*. Both outrank the Mission Score, which is Kaizen K1's explicit choice (`.294`): *"demoted `dashboard` behind week-recap … so Mission Score spills into Today details on the densest evening instead of crowding the session"*. Spilled, `TodayDashboardHeader` renders inside a collapsed `<details>` — present in the DOM, `hidden` forever. The failing snapshot shows it exactly: `group: Today details +`, with the band inside.

So on a Saturday after 18:00 the test read a deliberate product decision as a defect. Falsified both ways on one unchanged build: pinned to **19:30 it fails in 23.1s with `Received: hidden`** — the identical signature seen in the lane — and pinned to **09:30 it passes in 3.9s**.

**The part worth keeping is how convincingly it framed the wrong suspect.** It had passed on `master` (4.5s) and at `.608` (3.5s) and went red only with `.609`–`.611` open, so `.612` recorded it as "open and mine" and spent its investigation inside the diff. Every fact in that reasoning was true and the conclusion was still wrong, because "what changed in the repo" and "what changed in the world" are different questions and only one of them shows up in `git diff`. The tell was available the whole time and misread twice: it passed *in isolation* and failed *in the lane* — read as load-dependence, when the honest reading is that the two runs happened at different times and the isolated one raced `belowFoldReady`'s `requestIdleCallback(…, { timeout: 1200 })` before the evening blocks could mount. Two identical 23.1s failures were treated as evidence of determinism *in the code*; they were determinism in the calendar.

This repo had already paid for this once — `.211`, three cases with `2026-07-30` hardcoded that "passed all evening and failed the moment the clock rolled past midnight". That fix produced `fixedTimeAt`, which derives the day from the same clock the fixtures use, and it sat in `first-90.spec.ts` where a second spec could not reach it. It now lives in `tests/e2e/helpers/fixedClock.ts` with `UNCONTENDED_HOUR`, named for the property the caller actually needs — an hour at which the block budget is not contended — rather than for a number. `first-90.spec.ts` imports it instead of keeping its own copy; two copies of a clock rule is how the two drift apart.

**Not fixed by loosening the assertion.** The test still demands the band be *visible*, above the fold, showing a real number. It now just says which hour it is asking about. The evening layout stays covered where it belongs: `first-90.spec.ts` pins 9:00 **and** 19:00 and asserts the block budget at both.
