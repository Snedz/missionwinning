# Materials — Program 67 loft

Five materials. Continuity over catalog. No SKU. Do not invent a price.

Rooms that use them: [README.md](README.md). Vocabulary: [REDNOTE_SHIWO.md](REDNOTE_SHIWO.md).

## Oak

**Use.** Floor and millwork as one family so the loft reads as a single plane — stair stringer, island flanks, library uprights, wardrobe face. A real wear layer. Grain can be quiet.

**Skip.** Fake-oak vinyl sold as “the look.” Mixing three oak stains in one sightline. A floor that cannot take grit from the shoe closet.

## Limewash

**Use.** Soft light bounce on masonry or a wall that can take a mineral coat. Pairs with 2700K. Lets the industrial bones stay, without a gloss bounce that turns every cup into glare.

**Skip.** If the loft already has a sealed industrial coat you will not maintain. Limewash on a high-touch door plane that will spot. A “limewash” paint film that is just grey.

## Brass

**Use.** Small hardware — pulls, a kettle spout, a stair rail detail. One alloy, aged or living, not polished every week.

**Skip.** A gold explosion (every lamp, every tap, every frame). Thin plated steel that flakes. Mixing brass with chrome on the same island.

## Soapstone

**Use.** Island top that may patina — 容乱 at material level. Oil and a cup ring are part of the finish, not a defect. Sits next to the coffee fuel wall without asking for a showroom wipe.

**Skip.** If the brief wants a showroom shine (then oak or a honed stone you will actually live with). A thin veneer that chips at the kettle. Treating every mark as a failure.

## 2700K lighting

**Use.** Evening field on the island, library, stair treads, and wardrobe approach. Warm dim. One kelvin family so the loft does not change color at every door.

**Skip.** Gallery cool-white (office 4000K and up) on the living field. RGB strips. A different kelvin in every room “for interest.” Cooler task light is allowed on a work plane only — then it stays local.
